If you are trying to get more calls from Google, the two pieces that matter are Google Business Profile and Local Services Ads. They look similar on the results page, but they are separate products with separate logins, and they cost different things. Google Business Profile is free and earns your place in the local results. Local Services Ads are paid and buy placement above everything else. Set up the free one first, then decide whether the paid one fits your budget.

## Google Local Services Ads vs Google Business Profile: The Core Difference

Google Business Profile is where you show up for free. [MB Adv](https://www.mbadv.agency/google-business-profile/google-business-profile-vs-google-ads-key-differences) describes it as your organic presence on Search and Maps, with no account or listing fee. Google decides whether and where you appear based on relevance, distance and prominence, and you cannot buy a higher position.

Local Services Ads are a paid product. According to [Adnanagic's guide](https://adnanagic.com/blog/local-services-ads-vs-google-ads-roofers/), they appear at the very top of relevant results and can show a verification badge once you pass Google's checks. [MB Adv](https://www.mbadv.agency/google-business-profile/google-business-profile-vs-google-ads-key-differences) notes that Google replaced the old Google Guaranteed badge with a "Google Verified" badge in October 2025, so older articles may use the previous name.

### Why these are separate products with separate logins

Your Business Profile lives in its own dashboard and is free to create and verify. Local Services Ads run through a Google Ads account and need a funded budget. You can have one without the other, and many small roofers start with the profile and add paid placement later. Keep them in mind as two different tools with two different jobs, not one setting.

### Where each one appears on the results page

Local Services Ads sit at the top of the page, above the paid search text ads and above the local map pack. The Business Profile appears in the map pack, which is the block of three listings with a map, plus the Maps app and the business information panel. [The Diamond Group](https://www.diamond-group.co/blog/how-to-promote-a-roofing-business-on-google) points out that clicks from the map pack go to your profile rather than your website, so the profile has to make the first impression.

## How to Set Up Google Business Profile for Your Roofing Company First

Start here because it is free and it feeds everything else. Paid placement sends homeowners to the same profile, so a weak profile wastes money on any ad you buy.

### Categories and service areas

Choose a primary category that matches the work you actually do. The Diamond Group recommends "Roofing Contractor" rather than a broad label like "General Contractor" or "Home Improvement." Set your service area to where you truly work. An inflated radius makes the profile look less credible, and a mismatch between categories, services and areas weakens both your free and paid visibility.

### Photos and reviews that support the map pack

Upload photos of finished jobs on a regular schedule rather than in one batch. The same guide notes that photo captions with the service and city, such as "architectural shingle replacement in [city]," give Google more context than a generic caption. Reviews also count: the guide says Google uses review quantity, recency and diversity as ranking signals, so a steady stream of new reviews usually does more than a large old pile.

## How Local Services Ads Work for Roofers and What They Cost

Local Services Ads use a pay-per-lead model. You pay when a homeowner contacts you through the ad, and you do not pay for impressions or clicks that go nowhere. [Adnanagic](https://adnanagic.com/blog/local-services-ads-vs-google-ads-roofers/) describes this as the main reason the model feels more aligned with the outcome roofers care about. [Thomas Town Digital](https://www.thomastowndigital.com/blog/local-services-ads-vs-google-ads-roofing) adds that you go through a verification process covering your licence, insurance and background before the listing runs.

### Pay-per-lead model and verification requirements

Expect a verification step before you can run ads at all. Plan for the time it takes to gather licence and insurance documents. Because you pay per lead rather than per click, the budget is easier to forecast, but you will still be charged for leads that turn out to be poor fits. Thomas Town Digital notes that disputing poor-quality leads is possible but inconsistent.

### Typical cost per lead ranges

One 2026 data summary from [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/roofing-google-ads-statistics) puts roofing Local Services Ads at $75–$150 per lead, compared with $124–$228 for standard search campaigns. Treat that as one source's figure, not a benchmark. Your market, your service mix and your review count will move the number, so check what your own leads cost after the first month.

## Local Services Ads vs Google Ads: Which One to Run

Local Services Ads and standard Google Ads are often confused, but they are different buys. Local Services Ads are simpler and capture people who are ready to call now. Standard Google Ads let you pick keywords, write copy and send traffic to specific service pages.

### When LSAs fit a small roofing company

Local Services Ads fit a small company that wants leads without managing keywords or landing pages. Adnanagic notes they suit urgent repair calls well, where a homeowner with an active leak chooses from a short list of verified options. Thomas Town Digital says they capture the easiest, highest-intent leads but give you limited say over which searches trigger your listing.

### When standard search campaigns give more control

Standard search campaigns make sense when you want to push specific work, like full replacements or storm repairs, into particular areas. [Thomas Town Digital](https://www.thomastowndigital.com/blog/local-services-ads-vs-google-ads-roofing) lists granular targeting, control of messaging and the ability to scale up or down by season as the main advantages. They also require ongoing management, and poorly set up campaigns can burn budget quickly.

Several sources, including Thomas Town Digital and Webtonic, suggest running both, because Local Services Ads cover urgent local searches while search campaigns reach keywords the ads do not fully cover. Start with one, measure it, then add the second only if you have the capacity to manage it.

## A Simple Order of Operations for Roofers Using Both

Use this sequence so you do not pay for paid placement on a profile that is not ready.

1. Fix your Business Profile: correct category, accurate service area, current hours and phone number.
2. Upload recent job photos with descriptive captions and set up a steady review request routine.
3. Verify your company and check whether you qualify for Local Services Ads and what documents you need.
4. Start with one paid channel with a small budget and a clear monthly cap.
5. Review after 30 days and decide whether to add the second channel.

### Week-one checklist

Audit categories, service areas and photos first. Then check your Local Services Ads eligibility and gather licence and insurance paperwork. Both steps are quick, and they remove the most common delays.

### Tracking leads from each source

Track which channel produced each quote request, whether that is the Business Profile, a Local Services Ad or a search campaign. Note which requests turned into booked inspections and which jobs closed. That lets you judge cost against work won rather than comparing per-lead prices alone.
