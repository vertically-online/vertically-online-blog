Most small roofing companies don't have a lead shortage so much as a lead leak. Work comes from referrals one month and dries up the next, and the leads that do arrive often wait too long for a reply. Learning how to get roofing leads consistently means building several sources at once and then following up on each one. The steps below are ones a small company can start this month. For a broader view of the trade-offs, read [how to get more roofing leads and stop losing the ones you already have](/blog/how-to-get-more-roofing-leads).

## Start With the Leads You Already Have

Your past customers are the cheapest source you have. The catch is that referrals only happen if you stay in touch after the job ends. A short check-in, a photo of the finished roof, or a seasonal maintenance reminder keeps your name in front of people who already trust you.

Timing matters for the ask. GAF suggests putting a review or referral request in the [warranty packet homeowners receive after a job](https://www.gaf.That moment is when the homeowner is most pleased with the work.

Put a simple refer-a-friend offer in writing. A gift card or a discount on the next service is common, and you can track which referral converts to a signed contract.

## Build Trust Where Homeowners Search

### Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a local homeowner sees. Keep the hours, service areas and categories accurate. Post finished-job photos and short updates on a regular schedule, not just when you remember.

Local search is competitive, so treat the profile as ongoing work rather than a one-time setup. For the local tasks that matter most, see [setting up the local pieces of roofing SEO](/blog/roofing-seo).

### Reviews

Reviews are one of the strongest drivers of roofing leads. [Eagleview reports](https://www.eagleview.com/eagleview/get-roofing-leads/) that companies with 50+ Google reviews and a 4.5+ star rating tend to dominate local visibility and homeowner trust. You won't reach that number by asking occasionally.

Ask every customer at job completion. Send a direct link by text or email the same day, and reply to every review, positive or negative. Replies to bad reviews are read by future customers too, so keep them calm and specific.

## Use Neighborhood and Local Partnership Tactics

### Neighbor knocking after a job

When a roof goes on, the neighbors can see it. [Roofr suggests knocking on about 10 doors around a finished job site](https://roofr.com/blog/how-to-generate-more-roofing-leads), since homeowners see your truck, your crew and your work firsthand. It costs time, not ad budget, and it works best right after the crew finishes, when the work is visible.

### Partnerships with complementary local businesses

Trades that serve the same homes can refer customers both ways. [RoofSnap points to HVAC companies](https://roofsnap.com/blog/roofing-lead-generation-guide/) as an example: an HVAC tech on a new AC install can recommend a roof check, and you can return the favor. Homeowners' associations that keep preferred-vendor lists are another route worth asking about.

## Add Paid and Structured Channels When Ready

Paid channels make sense once your basics are working, because they amplify a process that already converts. Google Local Services Ads and Google Search Ads capture homeowners who are actively searching. Minyona says Google Ads capture homeowners at the moment of highest intent. Start with a small budget and track which keywords produce booked jobs, not just clicks.

Free inspections give homeowners a reason to pick up the phone. [GetJobber lists offering free roof inspections](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/roofing/how-to-get-roofing-leads/) as one of its twelve lead tips. Be clear about what the inspection includes and how long it takes, so the offer doesn't create extra unpaid work.

Lead marketplaces are another option. They can produce volume, so compare cost per booked job before you commit.

## Respond Fast and Follow Up Consistently

A lead that waits is a lead that moves on. [Eagleview notes that contractors who streamline quoting and follow-up can handle higher volumes](https://www.eagleview.com/eagleview/get-roofing-leads/) without sacrificing quality.

Most leads also need more than one touch. Minyona recommends a follow-up sequence of calls, texts and emails over time, because many sales take several touchpoints. Write the sequence down so it runs the same way every time, even on busy weeks.

## Weekly Checklist to Run

- Ask for two reviews from recent customers.
- Post one finished-job photo to your Google Business Profile.
- Follow up on every open quote from the past week.
- Track which source produced each booked job, so you know where to spend more time.
