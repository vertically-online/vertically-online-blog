Homeowners often decide who to call while they are looking at a leak, and your Google Business Profile photos are one of the fastest things they judge. If you are wondering what photos to upload to Google Business Profile for roofing, the short answer is real job photos first, then team and truck shots, and nothing stock or blurry. This guide covers the order to upload them, what to skip, and how often to refresh them.

## Why photos matter on your Google Business Profile

Your profile is often the first look a homeowner gets at your company. [ProLine's guide to Google Business Profile for roofers](https://useproline.com/google-my-business-profile-for-roofers/) puts it plainly: show your work with photos, and update them regularly to keep the profile fresh and visible.

Photos also signal that a business is real and active. [Bluecollarmarketinggroup's guide](https://bluecollarmarketinggroup.com/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) notes that project photos, team shots and clean jobsite images help a homeowner feel more confident before they call. A thin gallery of old or low-quality images does the opposite and can make a roofer look unproven.

## What photos to upload first for roofing

Start with photos of work you have actually done. Recent completed jobs carry the most weight, and you do not need professional photography to start.

### Job photos that show real workmanship

[Huddle Web Works](https://huddlewebworks.com/blog/the-first-three-photos-on-your-google-business-profile-decide-the-call) suggests photographing the job itself: a crew mid-tear-off, a clean stack of shingles staged before install, a finished roofline from the street, and a close-up of flashing done right around a chimney. Those shots show a homeowner that you have been on a roof.

Before-and-after shots are especially useful. The same source argues that a damaged roof next to the finished job does more to earn a call than paragraphs of copy. Social Spike's list of roofing photo types adds storm damage examples, flashing and detail repair work, and metal or flat roof projects, which cover the jobs homeowners often ask about.

[Local SEO Company](https://localseocompany.agency/blog/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) makes the same point about authenticity: use real roofing work rather than stock images, and show both the problem and the solution.

### Team, truck and equipment photos

Your crew in uniform, a branded truck and clean equipment help show that you run a real local business rather than an anonymous listing. [Local SEO Company](https://localseocompany.agency/blog/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) describes these as a signal of a professional operation, not a solo contractor presenting as a company.

A simple exterior photo of your office or shop, if you have one, also works. [Uvisible's roofing tips](https://www.uvisible.com/google-my-business-optimization-roofers/) list exterior, team and equipment photos among the types to include.

## Photos to skip or avoid

Some photos hurt more than they help.

- **Stock photos.** Real work builds trust, and stock images undermine it. Social Spike states that real job photos build trust and stock photos kill it.
- **Blurry or dark images.** [Huddle Web Works](https://huddlewebworks.com/blog/the-first-three-photos-on-your-google-business-profile-decide-the-call) says a bad photo tells a homeowner you do not pay attention to details, which is a poor message for a roofing business.
- **Faces you do not have permission to post.** Keep this in mind for crew members and homeowners alike. Get consent before a person appears in a shot, and skip any image where that is not clear.
- **Old photos that no longer match your work.** [Bluecollarmarketinggroup](https://bluecollarmarketinggroup.com/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) warns against photos that no longer reflect the work you do now.
- **Thin galleries.** Too few photos leave the profile looking underpowered, so a handful of strong, recent images beats one or two lonely ones.

Review your current gallery this week and remove or replace anything in these groups.

## How often to refresh roofing photos on your profile

Upload after every completed job if you can. [Huddle Web Works](https://huddlewebworks.com/blog/the-first-three-photos-on-your-google-business-profile-decide-the-call) recommends one photo per completed job, uploaded within a day or two, and notes that Google sorts owner-uploaded photos by most recent upload date. That makes the habit directly useful, because your newest work is what a homeowner sees first.

If per-job uploads are not realistic, [Local SEO Company](https://localseocompany.agency/blog/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) suggests adding new photos every month or two and setting a recurring reminder or handing the task to an admin person.

Pair new photos with regular posts and review replies. [ProLine](https://useproline.com/google-my-business-profile-for-roofers/) recommends posting weekly to stay visible, and Social Spike suggests Google Posts with a recent project, photos and a clear call to action. For more post ideas, see [Google Business Profile posts ideas for roofers](/blog/google-business-profile-posts-ideas-for-roofers).

There is no magic number of photos. [Huddle Web Works](https://huddlewebworks.com/blog/the-first-three-photos-on-your-google-business-profile-decide-the-call) makes the point that five recent photos from the last month tell a homeowner more than twenty from three years ago.

## A simple photo checklist to work through this week

1. Pick three recent completed jobs with clear before-and-after shots, taken in daylight and in focus.
2. Add one or two photos of your crew at work, keeping faces only where you have permission.
3. Add a photo of your branded truck or equipment.
4. Remove or replace any blurry, dark, stock or outdated images.
5. Check that your categories and service area match the work in your photos. For category guidance, see [best Google Business Profile categories for roofers](/blog/best-google-business-profile-categories-for-roofers).
6. If you have not set up the profile yet, follow [how to set up a Google Business Profile for roofing](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile-roofing) first.

## Recommendation for this week

Pick three real job photos from your phone and upload them today. Then scroll through your full gallery as a homeowner would, and remove anything stock, blurry or out of date. Make uploading one photo per completed job a standing task from now on.
