A homeowner who fills out a quote request may be talking to more than one roofer. Being first to give a real answer, and making it easy to book a visit, can help you get the job. An AI call after roofing quote request is one way to make that first answer arrive while the homeowner is still deciding.

## Why speed after a web quote request matters

A slow reply gives the lead time to call someone else. Quote requests often arrive when your team is busy or closed, so the first human reply can be delayed.

The goal of the first contact is simple: confirm the request, give the homeowner something concrete, and get an inspection on the calendar. Each step that waits is a chance for the lead to go cold.

## How the instant quote and the call work together

Vertically, the product this blog is published by, works on the roofer's own website. It is one option among several, so weigh it against what you already use.

### The instant estimate by email and text

When a homeowner submits a request, they can get an instant estimate by email with a branded PDF. They also get a text saying the quote is in their inbox, with your company details and how to reach you.

Your pricing is what drives the number, so set your labor rates, fixed costs, materials and markups before you rely on it. If you do not know how your prices should flow into an estimate, check how your current process handles it.

### The AI call about 5 minutes later

About 5 minutes after the request, an AI agent calls the homeowner on your behalf. It uses your company name and the homeowner's name and request details, so the call should sound like a follow-up on their request, not a cold sales call.

## How the call turns into an inspection booking

The AI agent can be connected to your Google Calendar and book an inspection directly. That removes the back-and-forth of "what times work for you" over several days.

### Booking against your calendar

Before you turn this on, decide which days and hours the agent may offer. A calendar with open slots you do not actually want to fill will create bookings you have to cancel, which damages trust with the homeowner.

### What the roofer gets back

When a booking is made, you get a confirmation text. The homeowner gets an email with the inspection details. Check both the first time you run it, to confirm the names, address and time are right.

## Keeping leads that don't book on the first call

Many homeowners will not book on the first call, and that is normal. A follow-up sequence messages the lead every several days over 28 days until they book an inspection or unsubscribe.

You also get a copy of the quote with a full lead breakdown, plus a mini CRM inside the email where you mark lead status. Update it after every conversation. A sequence that keeps messaging a lead you already spoke to is worse than no sequence at all.

## What a small roofer saw in practice

This is one client only: Arias Roofing & Siding in Sewell, NJ. Their results are not a forecast for your company.

- Leads rose from 1-3 a month, mostly referrals, to 7-10 a month from the website.
- Reply time fell from 24-48 hours to under 30 seconds.
- Jobs went from about 1 a month to about 5.
- They saved about 30 hours a week on quoting and lead back-and-forth.

Your numbers will depend on your market, your pricing and your local search presence. Before you expect similar results, look at how many web leads you get now and how long your first reply takes.

## How to check whether this fits your business this week

Answer these questions before you set anything up:

1. **What is your instant-quote ceiling?** Set a total above which the homeowner instead gets an email saying more information is needed. That keeps very large jobs from being misquoted.
2. **Which calendar and hours will the agent book into?** Block off time you do not want to sell.
3. **Who answers when a homeowner replies or asks for a person?** The agent is a first touch, not a replacement for your team.
4. **How will you record lead status?** Decide who updates the mini CRM and how often.
