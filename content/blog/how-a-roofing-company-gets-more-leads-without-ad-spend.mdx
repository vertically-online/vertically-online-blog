Most roofing companies that rely on referrals hit a ceiling. When referrals slow down, the pipeline goes quiet. This case study looks at a different route: how a roofing company gets more leads from the website it already has by responding to homeowners faster. The client is Arias Roofing & Siding in Sewell, New Jersey. Their numbers are one client's results, not a guaranteed outcome, but the steps they took are ones you can test this week.

## Where Arias Roofing & Siding started

Before the changes, Arias was getting roughly 1-3 leads a month, and most of them came from referrals. Replies to web inquiries took 24-48 hours. In that gap, a homeowner who had already requested a quote could easily call someone else.

## What changed on the website

Arias's website now has a quote page that asks service-specific questions about the type of job, the size and the materials. Homeowners who are not sure what they need get a guided flow instead of a blank form. That matters because a form that asks the right questions produces leads you can price and act on.

When a homeowner submits the form, they receive an instant estimate by email with a branded PDF. The email comes from the company's own address, so it reads as Arias, not as a third-party tool. The quote is built from Arias's own labor rates, fixed costs, materials and markups, so the number reflects how they actually work.

## Fast follow-up that kept leads warm

A quote alone does not book an inspection. Arias's homeowners also get a text saying the quote is in their inbox, with details on how to reach the company. About 5 minutes after the request, an AI call made on the company's behalf can book an inspection directly on the roofer's calendar, and the roofer gets a confirmation text.

Leads who do not book right away enter a follow-up sequence that messages them every several days for 28 days, until they book an inspection or unsubscribe. Follow-up is easy to forget when it is done by hand, and a sequence keeps it going without someone remembering each lead.

## The results: leads, speed and hours

Website leads rose from 1-3 a month to 7-10 a month, and the reply time dropped from 24-48 hours to under 30 seconds. Jobs went from about 1 a month to about 5. The company also saved roughly 30 hours a week that had gone into quoting and back-and-forth with leads.

## What you can copy this week

Start by measuring your own reply times. Pull the last 20 web inquiries and note how long each waited for a first response. Count how many went a day or more without an answer. That number is the leak you are trying to close.

Next, decide your instant quote ceiling. Set a job size above which a quote should not be instant, because very large jobs need more information before a price means anything. Below that line, decide what a fast, accurate first response looks like for your company.
