Storm season call overflow for roofing companies usually starts the same way. A hailstorm passes through on a Tuesday afternoon, the phones ring through the evening, and by the next morning the company that answered first has its schedule filling up. Perceptionist's April 2026 storm season piece describes how a single storm can generate hundreds of roofing calls in 48 hours, and that the contractors who answer first book the jobs. This checklist walks through what to set up before the storm, how to handle the surge, and how to follow up afterward.

## Why storm season call overflow hits roofers so hard

Storm calls arrive fast and in bunches. [Hashlogics reports](https://www.hashlogics.com/answers/how-do-roofers-handle-storm-call-surges) that call platforms serving the roofing trade see volume spikes of 300 to 500 percent within 72 hours of a major storm. The same source attributes to Goodcall's roofing data the finding that 62 percent of roofing calls go unanswered on a normal day. A two-person office that copes with a regular week can be overwhelmed within hours.

Homeowners also tend to call several contractors in a row and book whoever picks up. [Talkroute's analysis](https://talkroute.com/how-roofing-companies-handle-storm-surge-call-volume/) cites research finding that 68 percent of post-storm homeowners chose the first roofing contractor who answered live. That makes the first few minutes of each call more valuable than almost anything else in your week.

### How fast storm calls arrive

The surge front-loads. The first evening and the next morning carry most of the volume, then it tapers over about three days. Plan your coverage around that curve, not around an average day.

## Storm season call overflow checklist: before the storm

### Set up your phone and routing

1. **Decide who answers after hours and at peak.** Name the people, the backup and the time windows. signpost.com/blog/best-call-management-for-roofing-companies) describes overflow rules that forward calls to a backup number, an answering service or a virtual receptionist once main lines are full. Set those rules before the season, not during it.
2. **Test the overflow path.** Call your own main line several times at once and confirm where the second and third calls go. If they go to a voicemail nobody checks, fix that now.
3. **Keep a written availability list.** List which inspection slots you can offer and when. Staff who can give real dates convert calls faster than staff who have to ask someone.

### Prepare your intake script

Capture the details an insurance claim will need on the first contact. [Hashlogics recommends](https://www.hashlogics.com/answers/how-do-roofers-handle-storm-call-surges) collecting the carrier, the date of loss, whether a claim is already filed, visible damage and access notes. A caller who gives those details once is far easier to book than one who has to be called back.

Keep the script short. Under pressure, a long form slows everything down, so put the four or five must-have questions on one card by the phone.

## Storm season call overflow checklist: during the surge

### Triage calls fast

Separate active leaks from routine inspection requests before anyone is dispatched. [Hashlogics describes the same triage step](https://www.hashlogics.com/answers/how-do-roofers-handle-storm-call-surges): an active leak may need a tarp crew now, while an inspection request can take a scheduled slot. Mixing the two wastes crew time and delays the callers who need help most.

### Protect your existing jobs

Storm volume can pull attention away from customers you already have. Assign one person to keep active jobs moving, so current projects and crews are not left waiting while the phones take over.

Log every call with a status, such as new, booked, callback or lost, so no caller falls through the cracks. A spike strains phone systems and IT that were not built to scale, so a simple shared log matters more than it might on a normal week.

## Storm season call overflow checklist: after the surge

### Follow up on every unbooked lead

Storm leads need prompt attention. Run a follow-up pass within a day of the peak so no unbooked caller is left waiting. Send a text recap, offer the next open inspection slot and log each attempt.

### Review what the surge exposed

Look at which calls were missed and at which hours had the most volume. Use that to adjust coverage for the next event. If most missed calls came in after 7pm, your overflow plan needs evening coverage. If intake notes were thin, tighten the script.
