A hail damage roof claim settlement often comes in below the contractor's bill, and the homeowner is left wondering why. The gap usually comes down to two things: whether the policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value, and how old the roof is. Roofers who check those two details before writing a scope can avoid a stalled job. This guide covers what to look at first and what to do this week on open hail jobs.

## Start With the Policy Type on Every Hail Job

Ask the homeowner for the declarations page before you write anything. It tells you which settlement basis applies, and that decides which number the check will be built on.

Replacement cost coverage pays what a new roof costs today, minus the deductible. Actual cash value (ACV) coverage subtracts depreciation for the roof's age, so an older roof produces a smaller check. [Smart Insurance 101 explains the difference](https://smartinsurance101.com/homeowners-insurance-roof-damage-and-replacement-coverage/) and notes that many insurers apply ACV-only terms to older roofs even within otherwise replacement cost policies.

### Age-based depreciation on older roofs

Depreciation can be steep. Smart Insurance 101 states that [age-based depreciation can cut a payout by up to 50% on roofs over 20 years old](https://smartinsurance101.com/homeowners-insurance-roof-damage-and-replacement-coverage/). Treat that as a general example, not a promise about any specific policy. The point for roofers is that a low offer is often a depreciation issue, not a dispute over the scope.

## Sudden Damage vs Wear and Tear

Policies generally pay for sudden events like hail, not for age, wear or lack of maintenance. [Housing Info puts it simply](https://housinginfo.org/roofing/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-roof-replacement): hail and wind are almost always covered, while gradual deterioration almost never is.

### What the policy actually covers

Document hail impacts on the day you inspect. Photograph each affected slope, note the dents or bruising, and record the roof's overall condition separately. Keeping hail damage distinct from long-term deterioration in your photos and notes makes the claim easier to defend and makes your scope easier to check.

## What to Do This Week on Open Hail Jobs

1. **Get a written, line-item scope.** Itemize underlayment, flashing, drip edge, gutters and any code-required work. Missing items are a common reason offers come in low, and a line-item scope lets the homeowner and adjuster see exactly what is being priced.
2. **Put the settlement risk in writing.** Tell the homeowner that the insurer's payment may differ from your bill and explain depreciation before work starts. A short written note now prevents a surprise at the final invoice.

4. **Check whether depreciation is recoverable.** Some replacement cost policies pay holdback only after the work is finished and the final invoice is submitted. Confirm the timing before you schedule the crew.
