A roofing website checklist for UK roofers should start with one question: does each trust detail on your site match what you actually hold and offer? Visitors notice when a logo or guarantee is vague, and a clear, accurate page makes it easier to request a quote. ContractorUK, a company that builds websites for roofers, has a page on its website service covering the jobs to list, the schemes that matter and the legal details. It is a useful starting point, but note that it is a page from a website-building vendor's service.

This checklist works through the trust details to show, the ones to leave off, and what to fix this week.

## What the ContractorUK page says about roofer websites

The ContractorUK page focuses on websites for UK roofers and contractors rather than US roofing companies, so its specifics (such as the competent person schemes and building regulations) are UK-specific. It walks through the jobs to list, how to handle NFRC membership, and how guarantees and VAT details should appear.

## Trust details to show: certifications and schemes

Only show a certification if you hold it today. The [ContractorUK page](https://www.contractoruk.com/websites/websites-for-roofers/) notes that roofers registered with a competent person scheme can certify their own notifiable work, and it names CompetentRoofer and NAPIT as schemes listed on GOV.UK for replacing roof coverings. It also says customers of NFRC-registered roofers get a building regulations compliance certificate automatically.

### Competent person schemes and building regulations

- Show the scheme logo only if your membership is current.
- Link to the scheme's own find-a-member page so visitors can check.

- Follow each scheme's rules on using its logo.

If you are not a member of a scheme, say what you do instead, such as working with a building control route for each notifiable job. Do not imply membership you lack.

## Guarantees and job details to list

Guarantee wording should match what you actually offer, including the length and what it covers.

List the job types you do: repairs, flat roofing, slate, tiled, re-roofing and so on. Directories show this clearly. [Approved Roofers](https://approvedroofers.co.uk/) lists job categories such as new roofs, roof repairs, flat roofing, slate and tiled roofing, so visitors can see at a glance whether a roofer fits their project. Do the same on your own page, one section per service.

## Where directories fit and where your own site wins

Directories are useful for visibility. [Localroofer](https://localroofer.directory/) reports 10,128 roofing businesses and an average rating across its listings, and [Approved Roofers](https://approvedroofers.co.uk/) shows a 4.7 average rating and 3,200+ reviews. [Roofersaround](https://www.roofersaround.co.uk/) and [Construction](https://www.construction.co.uk/d_c/7/roofing-contractors) also list roofers alongside many others. These are context, not a substitute for your own page.

Your own site is where you control the details, the wording and the route to a quote. A directory profile shows what the platform allows. Your page shows what you want a homeowner to know. ## This week's action list

1. Audit your site for every logo, membership and certification. Keep only those you hold today.
2. Check your guarantee text against what you actually provide, and remove any length or coverage you cannot back up.
3. Compare your job types with what you really do, and add or remove sections to match.
4. Make sure each claim links to a verifiable source, such as a scheme's find-a-member page.
5. Remove any claim you cannot back up, even if it looks reassuring.
