Most roofers asking how to get more roofing leads already have a lead problem they can fix for free: they're losing the leads they get. So this guide comes in two parts. First, the lead sources worth your time. Then, the part most guides skip: how to stop those leads from slipping away.

## Part 1: How to get more roofing leads

### Your Google Business Profile

When a homeowner searches "roofer near me," your Google Business Profile is often the first thing they see. It's free, and it's the highest-return hour you'll spend on marketing.

- **Fill in everything:** services (replacement, repair, storm damage, gutters), service area, hours, and a real phone number that gets answered.
- **Add photos of real jobs** every week or two: before and after, crew on site, finished roofs.
- **Post updates** after storms, during busy season, and when you finish a notable job.
- **Answer every review,** good or bad, in a calm and specific way.

### Reviews, asked for every time

Reviews do double duty: they help you show up in local search, and they convince the homeowner who finds you. The simplest way to get more of them is to ask every single customer, right after the job.

Make it automatic. Send a review request by text and email the day the job is finished, with a direct link. Vertically does this for you after every completed job, but even a saved text template beats remembering to ask.

### A website that turns visitors into leads

Your website doesn't need to be fancy. It does need to make asking for a price easy:

- A **quote form** on every page, above the fold on mobile.
- A **click-to-call** phone number.
- **Photos of your work and your reviews,** because a homeowner checking you out after a referral will look.
- Ideally, an **instant quote**: the homeowner answers a few questions and gets a price right away. That's the moment they decide who to call back. See [how instant roofing quotes work](/roofing-quote-software).

### Google Local Services Ads

Local Services Ads appear at the top of Google for searches like "roof repair near me." Google says you ["only pay when potential customers get in touch through your ads"](https://business.google.com/us/ad-solutions/local-service-ads/), so you pay for leads, not clicks, and businesses that pass Google's screening and verification earn a Google Verified badge.

They can be a steady source of jobs. But the homeowner on the other end is often contacting more than one company, so these leads reward whoever responds first.

### Referrals

Referrals are usually your best leads, and you can get more of them on purpose:

- **Ask** at the moment the homeowner is happiest: on the final walkthrough.
- **Make it easy:** leave a few cards, and text them a link they can forward.
- **Thank people** who refer you, every time.

Even referred homeowners look you up before they call. A strong Google profile and website keep that referral from going to someone else.

### Storm response and canvassing

After hail or a big windstorm, homeowners need inspections quickly. Many roofers canvass affected neighborhoods door to door. If you do, check your local rules on door-to-door solicitation first, carry identification, and lead with a free inspection rather than a hard sell.

Storm weeks are also when your phone rings most. If you can't answer during a storm surge, you lose leads at the exact moment demand peaks.

### Partnerships

People who see roofs before homeowners call a roofer are a steady referral source:

- **Real estate agents** with listings that need roof work before closing.
- **Home inspectors** who flag roof issues in their reports.
- **Insurance agents** whose clients file storm claims.
- **Property managers** with multiple buildings to maintain.

Respond fast to their referrals, and they'll keep sending them.

### Job-site signs and truck wraps

A yard sign on every job (with the homeowner's permission) and a clean, branded truck put your name in front of the neighbors who just watched you work.

### Bought leads

Lead marketplaces can fill gaps, especially when you're starting out. The leads can be shared with other roofers, so they only work if you can respond within minutes. If you can't, you're paying for leads that go to your competitors.

## Part 2: Stop losing the leads you already have

Here's the uncomfortable truth: for a lot of roofing companies, the cheapest new leads are the ones they're already getting and losing.

### Speed to lead

Research published in the Harvard Business Review found that [most companies are not responding to online leads nearly fast enough](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads). In roofing it's harder still, because you're on a roof when the lead comes in.

Picture a homeowner who fills out your form at 2 PM. You see it at 8 PM and call back the next morning. By then they've often booked whoever got back to them first. You didn't lose that job on price or quality. You lost it on time.

The fix is a response in seconds, not hours: a reply that confirms you got their request, answers the first questions, and moves toward an inspection.

### Quote fast

The first question every homeowner asks is roughly what it'll cost. If your answer takes three days of back-and-forth, the homeowner keeps shopping. Even a first price within your limits, confirmed on the inspection, keeps you in the running. That's exactly what [instant roofing quotes from your own price list](/roofing-quote-software) are for.

### Book the inspection, not a callback

"We'll call you back" isn't an outcome. A booked inspection is. Whoever answers your leads, a person or a system, should end the conversation with a time on your calendar.

### Follow up more than once

Plenty of homeowners don't reply to the first message. Life gets busy. A short, friendly follow-up over the next few days recovers leads that would otherwise go quiet. If you don't follow up today, this is one of the easiest wins available.

### Answer the phone, even when you can't

Every unanswered call during a job is a lead that might not call again. An [answering service that books the inspection](/roofing-answering-service) means the phone gets answered and the visit gets scheduled while you keep working.

### Track where every lead comes from

You can't double down on what works if you don't know what works. For every lead, note the source (Google profile, website form, Local Services Ads, referral, yard sign, canvassing) and whether it became an inspection and a signed job. After a couple of months you'll know which sources pay for themselves, and how many leads you lost to slow responses.
