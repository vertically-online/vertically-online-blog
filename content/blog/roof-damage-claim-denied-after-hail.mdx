A roof damage claim denied after hail is no longer just a homeowner problem. The Oklahoma Insurance Department says State Farm improperly denied or failed to pay for roof damage from wind and hail, and a $45 million settlement now lets some affected homeowners ask for independent reviews. If you roof in or near that market, expect more homeowners asking what their denial means and what you can do about it. Start this week by listing your open insurance-claim jobs and tightening your damage documentation.

## What the State Farm settlement says about roof claims

The Oklahoma Insurance Department announced that an examination found State Farm improperly denied or failed to pay for roof damage and necessary repairs on wind and hail claims (KOCO 5 News). The settlement includes at least $35 million for affected customers, $10 million for the Strengthen Oklahoma Homes grant program, and $2.86 million in fines and exam costs (Yahoo! Finance, citing KFOR).

Homeowners with wind or hail claims that were closed without payment for dates of loss from January 1, 2021 to December 31, 2025 can request an independent review. The release's definition of "closed without payment" includes claims that received partial payment or were determined to be under the deductible amount ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/11/16/state-farm-agrees-to-45-million-settlment-with-oklahoma-for-claims-handeling/)). Oklahoma Watch reported that the examination found claims were closed without proper documentation and that damage was evident when no payment was made ([Oklahoma Watch](https://oklahomawatch.org/2026/11/16/state-farm-to-pay-45-million-after-oklahoma-insurance-investigation/)).

## What a roofer should do this week

### Check your open insurance-claim jobs

Pull a list of homeowners with denied or partially paid roof claims. Note the dates of loss and the carriers involved, since those details decide whether a claim falls in a review window.

Make sure your inspection photos and written findings are complete and dated before anything else changes. The independent administrator will evaluate the original claim file and any additional documentation the policyholder submits.

Tell homeowners that a denial can sometimes be reviewed, and that they should follow their insurer's process and state rules. Do not promise any outcome.

## Documenting hail and wind damage so it holds up

### What to record on the first visit

Photograph each slope, test squares and collateral damage such as gutters, vents and soft metals, with notes on location and date. Dated, labeled photos are far easier to defend than a summary written weeks later.

Keep your repair estimate separate from the claim scope so the two can be compared when a claim is disputed. Mixing them makes it harder to show what damage was found and what was priced.

Store copies of everything you send the homeowner so you can show what was delivered and when.

## Handling storm claim leads without overpromising

### Set expectations with homeowners early

A news settlement does not mean any individual claim will be paid. Avoid language that implies guaranteed approval, because each claim depends on the policy, the carrier's findings and the review outcome.

Ask which carrier, claim number and adjuster are involved so you know where the file stands. That one conversation keeps you from guessing about status later.
