A roofing lead that gets a quote and then goes silent is not necessarily a lost job. It is a lead that needs a steady, polite reason to come back. A roofing follow up sequence for leads that go quiet keeps that conversation alive over several weeks, so the homeowner has a clear next step each time instead of a quote that gets buried in an inbox. This post explains how that kind of sequence works, what it sends, and what to check before you turn it on.

## Why roofing leads go quiet after a quote

A quote may go unanswered for many reasons, such as comparing prices or waiting on a decision. That silence is not always a clear no.

The risk is timing. A quote sitting unanswered is easy to lose to whichever roofer follows up next. If you only follow up when you remember to, the leads that matter most often slip through.

### The gap between a quote and a booked inspection

The most valuable stretch of a roofing lead is the gap between the quote and the booked inspection. Closing that gap means reaching the homeowner again, with something useful, before they lose interest or hire someone else.

## How a 28-day follow-up sequence works

A follow-up sequence is a set of messages sent on a schedule, rather than one-off reminders you type by hand. With Vertically, the sequence sends messages to the lead every several days over 28 days, and it stops once the lead books an inspection or unsubscribes.

### Messages every several days

Spacing matters more than volume. Messages every several days give the homeowner time to think without the lead going cold. The sequence runs for 28 days, giving the lead several weeks to decide. The messages are automated and sent on schedule without you typing each one, even in a busy week when you are on a roof.

### When the sequence stops

The sequence ends in one of two ways: the lead books an inspection, or the lead unsubscribes. Either outcome is clean. You are not left wondering whether someone is still in the pipeline or whether you accidentally kept messaging a homeowner who already hired another company.

## What happens in the first five minutes and the call

The sequence runs alongside the first contact, so the lead is not waiting on a reply to start.

### Quote, text, then an AI call about 5 minutes later

When a homeowner submits a request, they get an instant quote by email, with a branded PDF that carries your details and sends from your own email. They also get a text saying the quote is in their inbox, with information about your company and how to reach you.

About 5 minutes after the request, an AI agent calls the homeowner on your behalf, using your company name and the details of their request. It is connected to your Google Calendar and can book the inspection directly. You get a confirmation text, and the homeowner gets an email with the inspection details.

## Setting up a follow-up process you can trust

Automation only helps if the inputs are right. Check these before you turn it on.

### What to check before you turn it on

- **Your pricing ceiling.** Set the threshold for instant quoting. If a job totals above it, the homeowner receives an immediate email saying more information is needed, so very large jobs are not misquoted.
- **Your service questions.** The quote page asks questions based on what you offer, so make sure they match your actual work.
- **Your calendar.** The AI agent can only book what your Google Calendar allows, so keep your availability current.

### Using the lead breakdown to track status

You receive a copy of each quote with a full lead breakdown and a mini CRM inside the email. Use it to mark each lead's status as it moves. That way, when a homeowner replies or books, you update the record once and the sequence reflects where things stand.

## Measuring whether the follow-up is working

You do not need a complicated dashboard. You need to know which quiet leads came back.

### What the weekly report shows

Every Monday you get a weekly report covering inspections, calls, quotes and requests. Read it with one question in mind: how many leads that were quiet last month booked an inspection this month?

Compare that with your own numbers from before you had a sequence. Use your own data rather than industry averages, since your territory, job mix and pricing will differ from anyone else's.
