A weekly lead report for roofing company owners turns scattered inbox activity into a short list of numbers you can act on. Instead of guessing why jobs slowed down, you can see where homeowners dropped off between the first request and a booked inspection. This guide explains what that report should cover, how to read it, and what to change once you have it. The goal is simple: find the step where leads stall, and fix that step this week.

## What a weekly lead report covers

A useful report answers four questions: how many requests came in, how many turned into quotes, how many led to calls, and how many ended in a booked inspection. Each number maps to a step a homeowner passes through. If one step looks weak, you have a specific place to look.

Vertically sends roofers a weekly report every Monday covering inspections, calls, quotes and requests. The point of a Monday send is timing: you see last week's results while you still have time to change how you work that week.

### Requests, quotes and calls

Requests show demand coming to your website. Quotes show how many of those requests produced an estimate. Calls show how many homeowners followed up with a conversation. Inspections show how many conversations became a booked visit.

Read the numbers in order. A drop at any one step tells you where the process breaks.

## Reading the weekly lead report numbers

Start with the gaps between steps, not the totals. A week with 20 requests sounds strong, but if only 3 turned into quotes, the problem sits between the request and the quote.

Here is a simple way to sort the most common patterns:

| What you see | Likely cause | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Many requests, few quotes | Quoting or pricing bottleneck | Whether the quote flow is confusing or slow |
| Quotes sent, few calls | Quotes not followed up | Time between quote and first contact |
| Calls made, few inspections | Booking friction or weak next step | How the call ends and whether a date is offered |
| Steady requests, falling inspections | Lead status not updated | Whether the mini CRM reflects real outcomes |

A high request count with few quotes points to a quoting or pricing bottleneck. Check whether homeowners are abandoning the form, or whether the estimate is taking too long to reach them.

Few calls after quotes suggests slow follow-up rather than weak demand. Homeowners who got a price and heard nothing for days often move on. Compare the time each quote went out with the time you first reached the homeowner.

## How a weekly lead report changes your week

A report is only useful if it changes what you do. Pick one stalled step and make one change before the next Monday. Changing five things at once makes it impossible to tell what worked.

For example, if quotes are going out but calls are not, you might decide to call every quoted homeowner within two business days. If requests are high and quotes are low, you might review the questions on your quote page and remove any that homeowners skip.

Also check that the data is accurate. Inspections booked should match the lead status you updated in the mini CRM. If the report shows bookings your CRM does not, or the reverse, fix the status habit before trusting the numbers.
