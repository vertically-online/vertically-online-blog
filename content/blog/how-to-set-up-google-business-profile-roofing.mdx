Knowing how to set up Google Business Profile for a roofing company is one of the cheapest local marketing jobs you can finish this week. The profile is free, and it controls how your business appears in Google Search and Maps. [Roofr's guide for roofers](https://roofr.com/blog/roofers-guide-google-business-profile) describes it as the place where customers see your business info, real photos and reviews without having to dig for them. The steps below cover what to enter, what usually goes wrong, and what to keep up after launch.

## Why your Google Business Profile matters for roofing

Many homeowners start with a local search. A complete profile gives them your phone number, hours, service list, photos and reviews in one place. [Gorizen's setup guide](https://blog.gorizen.com/roofers-google-business-profile) calls the profile one signal inside a broader local ranking system, so it works best alongside your website and local citations.

A profile does not rank by itself. It supports your local SEO, which is why it pays to get the basics right before you spend time on anything else.

## How to set up your Google Business Profile for roofing

### Create and verify your profile

Start at business.google.com and search for your company first. If a listing already exists, claim it instead of creating a second one. [ServiceTitan's guide](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/google-business-profile-roofing) notes that Google will prompt you to claim an existing listing when one already carries your business name.

Enter your business name, address and phone number exactly as they appear on your website and branding. Then request verification. Google usually mails a postcard with a PIN, which can take a few weeks. [Roofingbusinesspartner](https://www.roofingbusinesspartner.com/blog/how-to-optimize-your-roofing-google-business-profile) also notes that a profile can sometimes verify automatically if other online sources match your details, such as your website. Start the postcard process early either way, and check the mailbox for the card.

Use an email address you will always control for the account. [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/google-business-profile-roofing) recommends a business email rather than a personal one, so the profile stays manageable if staff change.

### Pick your category and service area

Set your primary category to Roofing Contractor. [Roofr](https://roofr.com/blog/roofers-guide-google-business-profile) recommends this for most roofing companies. If you offer related work such as gutters or siding, add those as secondary categories where they fit.

Because you work at the customer's property, mark that you deliver services at customer locations. [Roofingbusinesspartner](https://www.roofingbusinesspartner.com/blog/how-to-optimize-your-roofing-google-business-profile) recommends this option for roofers. Then define your service area by listing cities, counties or ZIP codes, or by setting a radius. Keep the radius to places you actually take jobs. A realistic 20 to 30 miles is easier to defend than a claim you have never served.

### Add services, hours and a description

List each service you sell, such as repair, replacement, inspection, metal roofing or tile. [Roofingbusinesspartner](https://www.roofingbusinesspartner.com/blog/how-to-optimize-your-roofing-google-business-profile) suggests listing all of them, since Google uses that list when matching searches.

Set your hours to when someone can actually answer the phone, and use a main business line. Keep the description short and factual: what you do, where you do it and what sets you apart, without keyword lists. [Oglinedigital](https://www.oglinedigital.com/insights/google-business-profile-mastery-for-roofers) makes the same point, noting that keyword stuffing hurts the reading experience.

## Keep your business information consistent

Your name, phone number, address and website should match everywhere they appear. [Roofingbusinesspartner](https://www.roofingbusinesspartner.com/blog/how-to-optimize-your-roofing-google-business-profile) warns that inconsistent naming confuses customers and can hurt SEO. [Roofr](https://roofr.com/blog/roofers-guide-google-business-profile) gives a clear example: if the website says "Brella Roofing LLC" and the profile says "Brella Roofs," both customers and Google can get confused.

Check the name on your website header, your directory listings and your social profiles. If you have many citations, a consistency tool can find mismatches faster than checking by hand. Fix the source listing first, then the copies.

## Add photos, reviews and ongoing updates

### Photos and posts

Upload your logo, crew photos and job-site images. [Roofr](https://roofr.com/blog/roofers-guide-google-business-profile) recommends before-and-after shots when you have permission to use them. Give image files descriptive names such as roof-replacement-chicago.jpg before uploading, since file names are a small signal Google can read.

Post updates regularly. [Oglinedigital](https://www.oglinedigital.com/insights/google-business-profile-mastery-for-roofers) describes Google Posts as a way to keep your profile active and engaging, and recommends regularly updating photos. A simple habit works: post after a finished job, and share seasonal notes before busy storm or repair seasons.

### Reviews

Ask customers for reviews steadily, not in one burst. [Oglinedigital](https://www.oglinedigital.com/insights/google-business-profile-mastery-for-roofers) recommends responding to every review, positive or negative, so readers can see how you handle problems. [Roofr](https://roofr.com/blog/roofers-guide-google-business-profile) also says to answer customer questions on the profile rather than leave them unanswered.

## Common setup mistakes to avoid

- **Creating a second listing.** If one already exists for your company, claim it. [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/google-business-profile-roofing) recommends claiming the existing listing when Google prompts you.
- **Setting a service area that is too broad.** A radius covering places you never work sends you calls you cannot serve and weakens relevance for the areas you do want.
- **Letting the profile sit.** Unanswered questions, old photos and stale hours all signal neglect.
