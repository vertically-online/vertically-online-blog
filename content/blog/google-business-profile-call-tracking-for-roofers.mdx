Google Business Profile call tracking for roofers starts with one problem: the profile gets the call, but nothing in the listing tells you which call turned into an inspection. For most small roofing companies, the profile is the front door for local searches, so its call volume matters. This checklist walks through setting up the profile so calls are measurable, connecting those calls to your pages and services, and reviewing the numbers each week.

## Why Google Business Profile Calls Are Hard to Track

Local search sends calls straight from the map results, often without the caller ever visiting your website. [PushLeads' guide to Google Business Profile for roofers](https://pushleads.com/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) describes the profile as the most visible digital asset a roofing company controls in local search, with the map pack collecting a large share of local clicks. That means a meaningful part of your inbound calls may never touch your website analytics.

Storm-season competition makes this harder. Roofingsites notes that out-of-state storm contractors often flood hail-damaged markets within days, and that a complete local profile with current photos and reviews is what helps your listing hold position. Calls can spike and then fall off quickly, so you need a weekly record rather than a memory of what felt busy.

## Checklist: Set Up the Profile So Calls Are Trackable

1. **Set Roofing Contractor as the primary category.** Social Spike's guide to GBP optimization for roofers recommends this as the primary category for most roofing companies, and it argues that secondary categories should only be added if they describe work you actually perform.
2. **Complete every section.** [PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) lists category, services, service area, hours and business description as the core sections. Empty sections give Google less to match against your listing.
3. **Add your services with short descriptions.** Each service you offer (repair, replacement, storm damage inspection, gutters) should be listed. A service-level breakdown also helps you later tell which call was about which job.
4. **Set up a service area if you have no walk-in location.** [ProLine's guide to Google My Business for roofers](https://useproline.com/google-my-business-profile-for-roofers/) explains that service-area businesses don't need a visible address, but you should list the towns and ZIP codes you actually cover. [Hustle Marketers' roofing SEO guide](https://hustlemarketers.com/roofing-seo/) says Google's guidance is that the service area shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time from where you are based, so do not list cities you can't realistically reach.
5. **Keep your business name, address and phone consistent.** Name, address and phone should match your website, your directory listings and the profile. Inconsistent details split your signals and make call attribution messier.
6. **Consider a tracking number as the primary phone.** Roofingsites suggests using a call-tracking number as the primary phone on the profile and keeping your long-standing number as the secondary. This preserves citation history while still letting you attribute calls.

## Checklist: Connect Calls to Pages and Services

1. **Build a page for each core service.** [GPITech's roofing SEO guide](https://www.gpitechusa.com/post/seo-for-roofers) recommends separate pages for each service and area. When a caller found you through a repair search, you can later check whether the call was about repair or replacement.
2. **Create location pages for each city you serve.** A page per area lets you match calls to where the homeowner is, which is useful for judging whether a service area is worth the marketing spend.
3. **Link the profile to a roofing-specific landing page.** Social Spike argues that a profile linked to service and location pages performs better than one linked to a generic homepage. This also gives you a clear path to see which visitors came from the listing.
4. **Collect reviews on a steady schedule.** [PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) recommends collecting at least 10 to 20 reviews before expecting strong map pack placement, and responding to every review. Reviews both support calls and affect whether the profile shows up at all.
5. **Upload real job photos.** Social Spike notes that before-and-after shots, crew photos and completed roofs build trust, while stock photos weaken it. Photos are part of what makes a caller choose your listing over a competitor's.
6. **Post updates weekly.** [ProLine](https://useproline.com/google-my-business-profile-for-roofers/) recommends weekly posts with a clear call to action so the profile stays active. Keep a simple log so you can compare call spikes against post dates.

## Checklist: Review Your Call Data Each Week

1. **Pull the profile call count every Monday.** The Google Business Profile dashboard shows calls, direction requests and website clicks. [PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) recommends checking these monthly at minimum; weekly is better in storm season.
2. **Map calls to booked inspections.** Compare profile calls with inspections booked from the same week. Your calendar and your CRM should be the source for booked inspections, not memory.
3. **Compare calls with quotes and web requests.** A call that never becomes a quote tells you something different from a quote that never gets booked. Keep the three numbers side by side.
4. **Log profile changes.** Note when you change categories, services, hours or the tracking number. A drop in calls is easier to explain when you know what changed that week.
5. **Watch for suspensions or listing problems.** [Hustle Marketers](https://hustlemarketers.com/roofing-seo/) lists common causes of suspension, including keywords in the business name, a visible home address, virtual offices and duplicate profiles. A suspended listing stops calls entirely, so check the profile status as part of the weekly review.

## Common Mistakes That Hide Call Data

- **A bare profile with no posts, photos or Q&A.** [PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/google-business-profile-for-roofers/) describes a bare profile as a common reason roofers fail to get calls, and an empty listing gives you very little to measure against.
- **Unmanaged listings that storm contractors crowd out.** Roofingsites warns that fake or keyword-stuffed competitor listings can grab calls during storm season. Keep your own profile complete and report obvious violations.
- **Changing the phone number without a plan.** Switching the primary number without recording the change breaks your history and makes it hard to tell whether a call drop is real.
- **Counting calls without counting outcomes.** A high call count with no booked inspections means your intake or follow-up is the problem, not your visibility.
