Does homeowners insurance cover hail damage to a roof? In most cases, yes. Standard policies typically cover storm damage from wind, hail, lightning, and the weight of snow or ice, while flood and sewer backup usually need separate coverage. After a storm, that distinction shapes what homeowners ask you, so it helps to know it well and to respond fast with a clear next step.

## What standard homeowners insurance usually covers on a roof

### Wind, hail, lightning and snow or ice weight

Standard policies generally cover storm damage to the roof from wind, hail, lightning, falling objects, and the weight of snow and ice. [Kin's guide to storm coverage](https://www.kin.com/blog/home-insurance-storm-damage/) lists wind and hail damage to roofs, siding, windows and gutters as typically covered, along with interior damage that follows when wind tears shingles off and rain enters the attic.

Coverage is not identical everywhere. [Fortune's explainer on storm damage](https://fortune.com/article/does-home-insurance-cover-storm-damage/) notes that some insurers apply separate wind and hail deductibles or limit coverage for older roofs, and that some insurers require additional coverage for certain storm losses. So a covered hail claim does not always mean a full replacement with no out-of-pocket cost for the homeowner.

## What homeowners insurance usually excludes

### Flood and sewer backup

Flood damage is the big exclusion. [Fortune's reporting on Hurricane Helene](https://fortune.com/2024/10/05/flood-insurance-hurricane-helene-homeowners-foreclosure-bankruptcy-wind-damage/) explains that most private insurers do not carry flood insurance, which leaves the National Flood Insurance Program run by FEMA as the primary provider for residential flood coverage. Check whether the policy covers water from rising water before assuming it does.

Sewer and drain backup is also commonly excluded. [COUNTRY Financial's storm coverage guide](https://www.countryfinancial.com/en/planning/common-topics/insurance-coverage/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-storm-damage.html) lists flood, earthquake and sewer or drain backup as storm-related losses that standard policies do not cover, and Kin says sewer backup typically requires an endorsement. If a homeowner describes water in the basement after a storm, do not assume the roof claim will pay for it.

## How the coverage question affects your roofing sales conversation

Coverage depends on the specific policy, so the homeowner should check with their own insurer. You are not in a position to say what a policy covers, and you should not promise an outcome on a claim. What you can do is explain the general pattern, then point them to their insurer and to your inspection findings.

Document the damage before you recommend repairs. Take dated photos of each slope, the gutters, and any soft spots or granule loss, and note the storm date if the homeowner knows it.
