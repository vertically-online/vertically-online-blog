A roofing quote is often the deciding moment in a homeowner's search. When a homeowner compares several companies, a fast, clear quote with a price that reflects your real costs gives you a better chance of being the one they call back. Roofing quote software can help, but tools differ a lot in what they actually control. Use this checklist to test any product before you pay for it.

Disclosure: Vertically is the author's product. It is one of the tools in the broader market, and this checklist applies to any vendor you evaluate, including ours.

## Why Quote Speed and Pricing Control Matter for Roofing Quote Software

Speed matters, but accuracy matters more. A fast quote built on a generic average will either lose you money or lose you the job when the homeowner's number changes at inspection.

### Why slow quotes lose jobs

Homeowners rarely wait around. If one company sends a clear estimate the same day and another takes a week, the first one is usually easier to remember and easier to compare against. The software does not need to be perfect, but the quote should arrive while the homeowner is still deciding.

Pricing has to reflect your own business. Your labor rates, fixed costs, material markups and crew size are what make a number honest. A price is only as good as the inputs you load into the tool, so the output is only as good as your price list.

## Checklist Part 1: Pricing and Measurement Features

**1. Custom price list and markups.** Confirm you can set your own labor rates, fixed costs and material markups. arrivy.com/blog/top-roofing-estimating-software/). If the tool only offers its own default pricing, walk away or plan to rebuild it by hand.

**2. How measurements are produced and checked.** Measurement methods vary. [RoofSnap describes satellite-based roof and gutter measurement reports with waste calculations](https://roofsnap.com/), while other tools rely on your crew's on-site numbers. Ask how the measurement is verified before a price goes out, and who is responsible if it is wrong.

**3. A ceiling for instant quotes.** Ask whether very large or unusual jobs can be routed to manual review instead of auto-quoted. A quote that is too low on a complex job costs you money, and one that is too high ends the conversation.

## Checklist Part 2: Customer-Facing Quote Features

**4. Instant quote on your website.** Several vendors offer self-quoting widgets. QuoteIQ's 2026 roundup describes a service-catalog quote builder embedded on a contractor's site. Decide whether you want homeowners to build their own estimate or whether you prefer to send them a proposal after you review the job.

**5. Branded proposal and PDF.** The quote should carry your logo, company details and contact information. Check that the email comes from your own address, not the vendor's. Homeowners who see a generic sender tend to hesitate.

**6. Contract and payment options.** Confirm whether contracts and online payment are included or cost extra. [RoofSnap lists contracts and online payment collection among its features](https://roofsnap.com/), but add-ons vary by vendor, so get the full list in writing.

## Checklist Part 3: What Happens After the Quote Is Sent

**7. Lead status and tracking.** You need to see which quotes are open, won or lost. Arrivy's review groups CRM and job management tools as a separate category from measurement tools. If the estimating tool does not track status, check whether it connects to the CRM you already use.

**8. Follow-up and inspection booking.** Check whether follow-up messages and inspection booking are included or whether you must send them by hand. A quote that sits without a follow-up often goes cold. For how the first minutes after a request play out, see [what happens in the first 5 minutes after an online quote request](/blog/what-happens-after-a-homeowner-requests-a-roofing-quote-online).

**9. Review request after the job.** Ask whether the software can send a review request when you mark a job complete. Reviews feed your Google Business Profile, which is a common source of local leads.

## Checklist Part 4: Cost, Trial and Fit

Compare the total cost, not just the monthly price. Setup fees, per-measurement charges and add-ons can change the math quickly.

Ask for a trial and read the cancellation terms before you enter a card. A trial only helps if you can test it with a real past job.

Fit depends on crew size. A solo roofer and a ten-person crew need different tools. [Arrivy notes that some platforms suit smaller teams while others are built for large multi-crew operations](https://www.arrivy.com/blog/top-roofing-estimating-software/), so match the tool to the work you actually do.

## Common Mistakes When Choosing Roofing Quote Software

**Buying for features you will not use.** A long feature list looks impressive, but most roofers need a reliable price list, a branded proposal and a way to follow up. Pay for those first.

**Choosing on a ranking alone.** Many published rankings come from vendors comparing their own products. Use them to build a shortlist, then decide based on your own numbers.

**Skipping the test quote.** Run one past job through each tool. Enter the same details you had at the time, then compare the output with what you actually charged and what the job cost you. The gap tells you more than any demo.
