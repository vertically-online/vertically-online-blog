Answering service pricing per minute vs per call is a choice that looks simple on a pricing page and gets expensive during a storm. A plan that fits a normal week can double or triple when hail hits and call volume jumps. Before you pick a model, run the numbers against your own call history, including the one weekend a year that matters most.

## Why answering service pricing per minute vs per call matters for roofers

Roofing demand comes in bursts. A single hail event can produce a flood of inbound calls in a couple of days, then drop back to a normal week. Pricing that looks reasonable on a quiet Tuesday can behave very differently on the weekend after a storm.

### Why normal-week call volume misleads you

If you size a plan using an average week, you will underbuy. Pull your last 12 months of call logs, mark the storm weeks, and price those separately from the rest.

## How per-minute answering service pricing works

Per-minute plans sell a block of minutes at a base price. Once you go past the block, each extra minute costs more than the base rate, so your effective price per minute climbs in exactly the weeks you can least afford it.

### Base plans and overage

[Hicira's 2026 price guide](https://www.hicira.com/resources/call-answering-service/answering-service-prices) gives a clear example: a plan with 100 minutes at $150 a month, used for 140 minutes, becomes $150 plus 40 extra minutes at $1.75 each, or $220. Overage rates in that guide run from $1.50 to $3.50 per minute.

### Where the bill jumps

Watch for these before you sign:

- Whether unused minutes roll over or expire.
- Whether after-hours or weekend calls cost extra.
- Whether a long call (for example, a homeowner describing a leak) bills at the same rate as a short one.

## How per-call answering service pricing works

Per-call plans charge for each handled call, usually with a monthly base on top. The unit is the call, not the minute, so a long call and a short call cost the same. That makes per-call pricing easier to predict for jobs where callers talk for several minutes.

### What counts as a call

Definitions vary. Some vendors count spam calls, wrong numbers and voicemails as billable calls. [Hicira notes that spam calls and wrong numbers count as calls too](https://www.hicira.com/resources/call-answering-service/answering-service-prices), and that some services filter them while many do not. Ask for the definition in writing.

## Run the math on your own call volume

Use one table to compare the three models against your numbers. Fill in your typical weekly calls, a storm-week multiple (use your own history, not a published figure), and each vendor's billing rule.

| Model | Billing rule | Normal week (your calls) | Storm week (your calls) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per-minute | Base + minutes over block × overage rate | Base + any overage | Base + overage at peak |
| Per-call | Monthly base + calls × per-call rate | Base + calls × rate | Base + storm calls × rate |
| Flat-rate | Fixed monthly fee | Fixed fee | Same fixed fee |

Then calculate the storm-week total for each model. Multiply your average call length by your storm call count to get minutes, and compare that to each plan's block.

## What to check before you sign a roofing answering service contract

Price is only one line of the contract. These questions will show you where the real costs sit.

### Billing questions to ask

- How are overage minutes or calls billed, and at what rate?
- Do storm-driven surges trigger a higher tier or a surcharge?
- Do minutes roll over, or do they expire at month end?
- Are extra phone numbers or after-hours coverage billed separately?

Get the answers in writing, and ask for a worked example using your busiest month.

### Trial terms and cancellation

Read the cancellation clause before the trial starts. [OnCallClerk's directory lists 14-day trials for several per-minute and per-call services](https://oncallclerk.com/blog/best-answering-services-roofing-companies). Check whether a trial converts to a paid plan automatically, and whether you can cancel without a notice period.
