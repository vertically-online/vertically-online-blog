When a homeowner requests a roofing quote online, the first few minutes decide a lot. Many people submitting a form are comparing several roofers at once, so the first reply often shapes which company gets the phone call. Knowing what happens after a homeowner requests a roofing quote online lets you check whether your own process is fast enough to keep the lead.

This post walks through the sequence that should fire after a web request, in order, so you can test your setup against it.

## Why the first few minutes matter after a quote request

A homeowner who fills out a form usually has not committed to anyone yet. Whoever responds first while their project is still fresh has an advantage.

Slow replies are a common reason leads go cold. Before it used Vertically, Arias Roofing & Siding (Sewell, NJ) was often taking 24-48 hours to reply to online requests.

## The instant quote and confirmation

### What the homeowner receives right away

A well-built system answers while the homeowner is still on the page or minutes later, not the next business day. Here is what they should get:

- **An instant estimate by email.** It includes a branded PDF and is sent from your own email address, not a generic sender.
- **A "more information needed" email for large jobs.** If the job total is above the ceiling you set for instant quoting, the homeowner gets an immediate note saying your team needs to review the project. This keeps very large jobs from being misquoted.
- **A text message.** It tells them the quote is in their inbox and how to reach your company.

If any of these steps depends on someone remembering to send something, it is a gap. Decide now which of these fire automatically.

## What the roofer receives at the same time

You should get a copy of every quote the moment it goes out. A useful version includes:

- A full lead breakdown: service type, size, materials and the answers the homeowner gave.
- A mini CRM inside the email, where you can mark the lead's status without logging into another tool.

Pricing should come from your own numbers, not a generic average. That means your labor rates, fixed costs, materials and markups. If a tool can't reflect those, the instant quote is only a rough guess you will have to redo by hand.

## What happens after the first five minutes

The first hour is not the end of the sequence:

- **A follow-up over 28 days.** The lead gets messages every several days until they book an inspection or unsubscribe. Homeowners who request a quote often are not ready the same day, so this matters.
- **A Google review request.** When you mark a job completed in the mini CRM, the customer gets an automated email and text asking for a Google review.
- **A weekly report every Monday.** It shows inspections, calls, quotes and requests, so you can see where leads are moving.

## How to check your own first-five-minutes process

You can test your current setup this week:

1. Submit a test request on your own website. Write down the time.
2. Note when the instant quote arrives, when the text arrives, and when anyone first contacts the lead.
3. For each step, ask whether it happens automatically or depends on someone remembering. Wherever it depends on memory, assign a named owner, or automate it.
4. Compare your result with Arias's experience: their reply time dropped from 24-48 hours to under 30 seconds. That is one client's reported result, not a guarantee for your business.
