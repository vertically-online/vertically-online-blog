Instant quotes only help if the number is close to what your crew will actually charge. Roofing quote software with your own price list fixes that by building every estimate from your numbers, and a ceiling for large jobs keeps very big projects out of automatic quoting.

## Why instant quotes go wrong

Your crew rates, your material markups and your fixed costs are what make your bids profitable, and an average knows none of them.

A wrong number at the start costs you trust. When the inspection reveals a different scope or a higher price, the homeowner feels misled, even if the first estimate was never meant to be final. Fixing that gap after the fact is harder than getting it right in the first email.

## How a price list keeps instant roofing quotes accurate

An accurate instant quote starts with a price list that reflects how you already bid work. That means your own labor rates, your fixed costs, your material pricing and the markups you apply. When the estimate is built from those inputs, the number matches the logic you use on paper.

### What goes into your price list

Start with the work you actually sell. List each service, such as tear-off, re-roof, repair, or siding, and attach the labor rate, fixed costs and material markups you would normally apply. Keep the list to what you quote regularly so it stays easy to maintain.

Custom questions for each service feed the calculation. Ask about the service type, the size of the roof and the materials the homeowner wants. A guided flow helps homeowners who do not know their roof details, so the answers you get are usable without a phone call first.

### How the homeowner's answers change the number

Each answer should move the estimate in a way you would recognize. A larger roof, a steeper pitch or a premium material should change the total the same way it changes your bid. If a homeowner picks a material, the price list should apply the right cost and markup for that material.

Test a few combinations yourself before you go live. Pick a small repair, a mid-size re-roof and a full replacement, and check whether the totals look like what you would quote by hand.

## Set a ceiling for jobs that need a human

Some jobs are too large or too unusual to quote automatically. A ceiling sets the total above which the instant quote is replaced by an immediate email saying more information is needed. That way very large jobs are not misquoted, and the homeowner knows the next step right away.

### Choosing your instant-quote limit

Pick a number based on the jobs you are comfortable pricing from the form alone. Look at your recent invoices and find the point where a job starts needing a site visit, a custom scope or a conversation about materials.

Review the ceiling by job type. A limit that fits small repairs may be too low for full replacements, and a limit that fits replacements may be too high for repairs that carry more risk. Adjust it as you learn where your estimates hold up.

### What the homeowner receives for a large job

When a job is above the ceiling, the homeowner gets an email saying more information is needed rather than a number that may be wrong. The message should say what happens next, so the request does not feel ignored. Your follow-up then becomes a sales conversation based on the real scope.

## What to check before you trust the number

Before you rely on instant quotes, confirm the inputs. Check that every labor rate, material markup and fixed cost matches your current bids. Old rates are the most common source of drift.

Then compare results to real jobs. Take the first 10 generated quotes and set them against the final invoices. Where the quote and the invoice differ, find the input causing the gap and fix the price list. Repeat this check every few months, or whenever your material costs shift.
