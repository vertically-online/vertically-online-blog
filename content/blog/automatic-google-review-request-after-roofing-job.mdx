An automatic google review request after roofing job completion is one of the simplest ways to keep your reviews current. The idea is straightforward: when a job is marked finished, the customer gets a short message asking for a review, while the work is still fresh in their mind. When the crew moves straight to the next job, the request is easy to forget.

This guide covers when to send the request, how to set it up, what to put in the message, and the mistakes that cause trouble. Done right, it runs without anyone remembering to follow up.

## Why Review Timing Matters for Roofers

A customer who has just watched their roof go on is usually happy, and that is the moment to ask. If you wait weeks, the crew is gone, the cleanup is forgotten, and the customer is less likely to bother with a review at all.

Manual reminders also fail under load. During a busy month, someone has to remember which jobs closed, look up the customer's contact details, and write a message. That step gets skipped first.

### When customers are most willing to leave feedback

Ask as soon as the job is truly finished, meaning the final walkthrough and cleanup are done and the customer has the paperwork. That is when their satisfaction is highest and the experience is clearest.

## How to Set Up an Automatic Review Request

The setup has one trigger and one action. The trigger is a job status change. The action is a message sent to the customer.

### Mark the job complete in your system

Use a single status for completion, and only change it after final sign-off. If your job tracking has a separate "work done, punch list open" stage, do not treat that as complete. The review request should fire from the status that means the job is closed.

### Send the email and text together

Send the request by email and text at the same time. Sending both channels covers customers who prefer text and those who check email. Make sure both messages carry your company name so the customer recognizes the sender.

## What to Include in the Review Message

Keep it short. A customer should understand the request in a few seconds and be able to act on it in under a minute.

### Keep the ask short and specific

Name the customer and the work they had done, such as a full tear-off and replacement or a storm repair. A specific line reads as a real message, while a generic one can look like spam.

### Link straight to your Google review page

Link to the page where the customer can leave a review directly, not your homepage. Every extra click loses people. You can find your review link from your Google Business Profile dashboard. Here is a simple structure:

- One sentence thanking the customer for the work
- One sentence naming the job
- One direct link to your review page
- Your company name and a way to reply

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Asking before the job is actually finished

A request sent before the final walkthrough can annoy a customer who still has a problem. Trigger the request only after final completion, and keep the status change honest.

### Sending the same message to every customer

Identical messages are easy to ignore. Vary the job name and details, and keep the wording plain. You do not need many versions, just enough that the message reads as written for that customer.

### Incentivizing or pressuring for positive reviews

Do not offer discounts or prizes for reviews, and do not ask customers to leave only good ones. Ask every customer the same way and let them decide what to write. Pressure on reviews can damage trust with customers.

## How Review Requests Fit Your Lead Follow-Up

Review requests and lead follow-up should sound like they come from the same company. Use the same business name, phone number, and contact details across both. A customer who got a quote and then a review request should recognize both as yours.

Track which completed jobs have already received a request. Without a record, the same customer can get asked twice, which looks careless. A simple status field or a column in your job list is enough. ## A Simple Weekly Check

Once a week, pick one time, such as Monday morning, and run through two checks.

### What to look at each Monday

- Count completed jobs against review requests sent. Any gap means a trigger was missed or a status was not updated.
- Check new reviews and reply to each one. Short, specific replies that name the work done are enough.
