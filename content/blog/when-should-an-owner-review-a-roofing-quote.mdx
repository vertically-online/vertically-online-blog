Most roofing quotes look alike on the surface. One line says "full replacement," another says "new roof," and the totals can differ by thousands. The question for a roofing business owner is when to review a roofing quote yourself before it reaches the customer, rather than trusting it to whoever built it. This checklist gives you the specific moments and conditions that should trigger your review.

## Why Owner Review Matters Before a Quote Goes Out

A quote is a promise. Once a homeowner signs it, every gap in scope becomes a dispute, a change order or a margin hit. Homeowner guidance on comparing bids makes the same point: a number without supporting detail is hard to evaluate, because it does not show whether two contractors are pricing the same job. Rescreeningmasters' guide to roofing quote questions explains that a low bid may omit tasks or rely on assumptions that later become extra charges.

If customers are already comparing you against other bids, your quote has to hold up line by line. Owner review is where you catch the gaps before the customer does.

## Checklist: Scope and Line Items to Confirm

Start with the scope. Before any quote goes out, confirm that the document states what is and is not included.

### Tear-off and disposal

- Confirm whether the job is a tear-off or an overlay. An overlay is only appropriate in specific conditions, and [Equityroofs' comparison guide](https://blog.equityroofs.com/roof-quote-comparison-mistakes) notes that skipping tear-off is a common way quotes get kept low. If the quote is an overlay, you should be able to say why.
- Check that disposal, permits and ventilation are explicitly listed. [Pcroofingservice's Bay Area checklist](https://pcroofingservice.com/roofing-quote-checklist/) argues that a quote without a permit line item or a decking allowance looks cheaper only until change orders arrive.

### Decking and hidden damage

- Check how decking repair is handled. Rescreeningmasters recommends a written method, such as a per-sheet price or unit pricing, rather than a vague promise to replace damaged wood.
- Confirm exclusions and who handles deck damage found after tear-off. If the quote says nothing, the customer will assume the cost is included.

## Checklist: Pricing and Materials Triggers

Pricing review has two parts: the materials description and the sanity check against your own numbers.

### Materials and manufacturer details

- Review any quote whose materials are not itemized by type, manufacturer and color. [Equityroofs](https://blog.equityroofs.com/roof-quote-comparison-mistakes) recommends listing roofing materials this way, and a missing brand or color invites a comparison you cannot win.
- Check underlayment. [Elite-roofs' second-opinion guide](https://elite-roofs.com/roof-estimate-second-opinion/) suggests asking whether underlayment type and amount are clearly stated, along with drip edge, ridge components, ventilation and flashing.

### Large or unusual jobs

- Flag quotes that sit above your own instant-quote ceiling. A job above that ceiling should not go out as an instant number, because it needs more information first. If you have not set that ceiling yet, [how to choose a roofing quote review limit](/blog/how-to-choose-a-roofing-quote-review-limit) walks through the decision.
- Flag quotes that depart sharply from your price book. If your per-square pricing says one thing and the quote says another, find out why before it is sent. [A roofing price book template per square](/blog/roofing-price-book-template-per-square) can help you check the numbers consistently.

## Checklist: Terms and Customer-Facing Risk

The last review is about the terms, because these are where disputes usually start.

- Check payment terms, change order language, warranty and interior damage coverage. Equityroofs lists these as project terms that belong in the estimate, including down payment requirements and whether the contractor will register the roof warranty.
- Confirm who is responsible for the decision. [Roof Crafters](https://www.roof-crafters.com/learn/roofing-quotes-find-them-does-insurance) explains that whether the homeowner or an insurer is responsible for obtaining quotes depends on the situation and the policy. When an insurer is involved, the scope you sign off on may not be the scope that gets approved, so note that in the quote.

Large residential decisions often involve more than one signer. If a quote is waiting on someone other than the homeowner, make sure the document is written so that person can compare it without calling you for clarification.

## How to Build a Review Step Into Your Workflow

Owner review only works if it happens every time. A written trigger list does that. It tells staff which quotes come to you before they go out, so the decision does not depend on whoever happens to be in the office.

A practical trigger list might include:

1. Any quote above your instant-quote ceiling or flagged for more information.
2. Any quote with an overlay, a decking allowance or a permit exclusion.
3. Any quote where materials are not itemized by type, manufacturer and color.
4. Any quote that differs from your price book by a set percentage you choose.
5. Any quote where the homeowner has said they are comparing against other bids.

Keep the list short enough that your team will follow it. For the last item, use a second-opinion mindset. Customers often compare estimates, so be ready to explain each line. If you can justify a number, the comparison usually works in your favor. If you cannot, the quote needs more work before it goes out.

## Common Mistakes Owners Make When Reviewing Quotes

**Reviewing only the total.** A total tells you the price, not the scope. Equityroofs warns against comparing prices before confirming both quotes cover the same work. Check exclusions and permit language before you look at the number.

**Letting repeat customers skip the scope check.** A returning customer or a referral can feel low-risk, and the temptation is to send a quote from memory. Scope changes from job to job, so the check should run every time.

**Treating a fast turnaround as a reason to skip review.** Speed matters, but a fast wrong quote costs more than a slightly slower right one. Use the trigger list so speed and review can coexist.
