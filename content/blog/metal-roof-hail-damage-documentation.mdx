A hailstorm can leave a metal roof dented, scratched or with a damaged coating, and the owner's insurance claim often depends on what was recorded before anyone started repairs. Good **metal roof hail damage documentation** protects the owner's options and makes your own inspection report more useful. This guide covers what to capture, why dents get disputed, and how to make the process easier for owners. It is general information, not advice on any specific policy or claim.

## What to do on a metal roof before any repair

Repairs can erase hail evidence, so the first job is recording the roof as it is. Before anyone pulls a panel or touches a seam, photograph the panels, seams, fasteners and coating. Shoot wide shots of each slope and close-ups of any impact marks, with a scale reference where you can.

### Photograph the evidence first

A dent is hard to prove later if the only record is a memory. Take dated photos and video from the ground and, if it is safe, from the roof. Note which panels face the storm direction, and record any cracked factory paint, lifted fasteners or displaced seams you find.

Also note the storm date and collect any storm records the owner can get, such as weather service reports or hail maps for that address. The owner may need these for their file, so ask them to save the originals.

## Why cosmetic dents get disputed

### The cosmetic exclusion question

Many policies carry a cosmetic damage exclusion, which can mean dents alone are not paid. Policy wording varies, so check the language with the owner rather than trying to interpret it in your report. Your job is to document the roof, not to decide what the policy covers.

Dents can still matter if they affect function or lead to premature wear. [A public adjuster's guide on cosmetic exclusions](https://www.coatsclaimsconsulting.com/post/hailstorms-vs-metal-roofs-beating-the-cosmetic-exclusion) explains that impacts beside fasteners or across seams can let water in, which is the question a careful inspection should address. Look for that kind of functional evidence rather than only counting dents.

The [Insurance Claim Recovery Support guide on metal roofing hail claims](https://insuranceclaimrecoverysupport.com/what-property-owners-must-know-about-metal-roofing-hail-claims/) draws the same line: cosmetic damage changes appearance, while functional damage impairs the roof's ability to keep water out.

## What an inspection report should include for hail claims

A strong report separates what you observed from what you think it means for coverage.

### Findings that help the owner's file

Describe panel, seam, fastener and coating condition with photos. Measure the hail impacts where you can, and note their location relative to fasteners and seams. Record any interior signs of water, such as stains or wet insulation, with the date you saw them.

Keep the report factual. State what you observed and leave coverage conclusions to the insurer, the owner, or a licensed professional. A report that reads as neutral evidence is more useful to everyone.

## How roofers can make this easier for property owners

A clear packet in the first week helps them.

### Simple steps in the first week

Give the owner a dated photo set and a written inspection summary they can send to their insurer. Include the storm date, the roof details and a list of what you found. Tell them to keep the original files and any repair receipts.

If the claim is complex or has been denied, refer the owner to a licensed public adjuster rather than arguing coverage yourself. Ask the owner to confirm any adjuster's credentials and fee terms in writing before signing anything.
